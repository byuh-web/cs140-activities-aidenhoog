A Brief History of Chanel

Introduction

Founded over a century ago, Chanel is one of the most recognized and influential luxury fashion houses in the world. The brand revolutionized women's fashion by prioritizing comfort and simplicity over the restrictive styles of the early 20th century, forever changing how women dress.

The Early Years (1910–1920s)

  • 1910: The brand was founded by Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel when she opened a small millinery (hat) shop called Chanel Modes at 21 rue Cambon in Paris.
  • 1913–1915: Chanel expanded, opening boutiques in the coastal resort towns of Deauville and Biarritz. Here, she began designing casual wear using jersey—a comfortable, affordable fabric previously used mainly for men's underwear. This marked a radical departure from the corseted silhouettes of the era.
  • 1921: The brand launched Chanel No. 5, the first fragrance to ever feature a designer's name. Its minimalist, geometric bottle design remains an icon of modern branding.

Iconic Innovations (1920s–1950s)

  • 1925: Chanel introduced the now-legendary Chanel suit, characterized by its collarless tweed jacket and well-fitted skirt. It offered women a blend of elegance, comfort, and mobility.
  • 1926: The introduction of the "Little Black Dress" (LBD). Before Chanel, black was strictly reserved for mourning. She transformed it into the standard for chic, versatile evening wear.
  • 1939–1954: At the outbreak of World War II, Chanel closed her couture house. She remained retired until 1954, when, at the age of 71, she staged a highly successful comeback to counter the ultra-feminine, restrictive styles that had become popular in the post-war years.
  • 1955: The brand released the 2.55 handbag (named for its release date, February 1955). Featuring a revolutionary shoulder strap, it allowed women to keep their hands free, combining practicality with luxury.

The Modern Era (1980s–Present)

  • 1971: Coco Chanel passed away at the age of 87. The brand continued under various designers for a decade.
  • 1983: Visionary designer Karl Lagerfeld was appointed as Artistic Director. Lagerfeld successfully modernized the brand for a new generation while preserving Coco Chanel's original design language—incorporating tweed, pearls, and the interlocking "C" logo into fresh, contemporary designs.
  • 2019 to Present: Following Lagerfeld's passing in 2019, Virginie Viard (his longtime right-hand collaborator) took over as Artistic Director, continuing the house's legacy. Today, Chanel remains a privately held company owned by the Wertheimer family and stands as a global symbol of timeless luxury.
